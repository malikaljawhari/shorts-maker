# Copyright, rights, and monetization — practical overview

This is a research/checklist guide, **not legal advice or a guarantee of earnings**.

## Rights checklist, before every video

- [ ] Identify who owns the source video and whose gameplay/game assets are shown.
- [ ] Confirm permission **to reuse/edit/publish**, including monetization rights, or have a properly considered exception under applicable law. Download rights under the **source platform's Terms of Service** are a separate question.
- [ ] Check original soundtrack, background music, game radio, voice clips and any other third-party materials.
- [ ] Document permission, restrictions and original URLs/dates privately in a copy of `rights_log_template.csv`.
- [ ] Make genuinely original analysis or entertainment value rather than merely cropping/re-captioning the source.
- [ ] Verify facts and quotes. Label speculation as speculation.
- [ ] Before public upload, use YouTube's copyright checks and review the results; checks are not a final legal clearance.

**YouTube can deny monetization for reused or mass-produced/inauthentic content even if there is no copyright strike.** Commentary and editing can support originality but do not automatically create a copyright exception. See [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392).

## Practical footage paths

Best path: capture your **own** permitted footage and write your **own** script. Alternatively obtain original source files from a creator who has documented sufficient redistribution permission, while checking publisher/music rights. Official game announcements may be useful for research; do not assume a public trailer is licensed for commercial reposting. Avoid unauthorized leaks.

A YouTube URL field is a convenience, **not** authorization. YouTube's [download help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/56100) specifies how users can download their own uploads; do not assume permission to download somebody else's content via technical tools.

## YouTube Shorts Partner Program: thresholds verified November 2026

- **Earlier YPP access in eligible regions:** 500 subscribers plus 3 public uploads within 90 days, and either 3 million eligible public Shorts views within 90 days or 3,000 valid public watch hours in the last 12 months. This is for selected additional features, **not** general Shorts ad sharing.
- **Full ad-sharing threshold:** 1,000 subscribers and either 10 million eligible public Shorts views in 90 days or 4,000 valid public long-form watch hours in 12 months; acceptance and proper terms are also required.
- **New condition starting February 1, 2027:** YouTube announced an ongoing 10-million-qualified-Shorts-views-in-90-days threshold for monthly Shorts revenue-sharing eligibility. Read the latest official policy because it can change.
- Being eligible does not guarantee approval; the entire channel is reviewed for policy compliance.
- **If under 18**, eligible AdSense for YouTube monetization requires a parent/guardian 18+ with an approved account linked through YouTube Studio. Fan-funding features may have their own age minimums.

Official links: [YPP overview](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851), [expanded YPP](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240), [Shorts monetization](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220), [AdSense under-18 guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2533300).

## Income expectations

No fixed payout per million Shorts views. It can take a long time to meet thresholds, and early channels often earn nothing. Do not plan subscription spending on optimistic view forecasts. Before YPP, use the channel to learn production and measure engagement. Evaluate only legitimate, age-eligible commercial opportunities. Never artificially inflate views with bots, bought traffic or engagement groups.

## Synthetic voices and disclosure

`edge-tts` provides word timing but uses an unofficial connection to a third-party voice service. **The code's MIT license does not grant voice commercial-use rights.** Verify the actual voice/service terms before publishing monetized content, or use your own recorded narration or properly licensed speech service. Do not mimic an identifiable creator's voice without consent. Follow any platform disclosure requirements that apply to realistic synthetic media.
